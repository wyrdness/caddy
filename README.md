# Cadborosaurus - Caddy, The Pacific Northwest Sea Serpent

## Category: CRYPTID

## Version: 1.0.0
**Last Updated:** 2026-01-14

## Overview

**Cadborosaurus willsi** (nicknamed "**Caddy**") is a sea serpent cryptid reported from the coastal waters of the Pacific Northwest, primarily in British Columbia's Strait of Georgia, Cadboro Bay (from which it gets its name), and surrounding waters including Puget Sound, Washington. First formally named in 1933 by newspaper editor Archie Wills (hence *willsi*), the creature has been sighted for centuries, with dozens of reports from credible witnesses including fishermen, sailors, marine biologists, and tourists.

Described as a long-necked, serpentine marine animal measuring 15-60 feet in length, Caddy is characterized by a horse-like head, long neck, humped body, and possible flippers or fins. Unlike purely legendary creatures, Caddy has been photographed (though inconclusively), and alleged carcasses have been examined (all proven to be known animals upon analysis). The creature shares characteristics with other sea serpent reports worldwide, leading cryptozoologists to speculate it may represent an unknown species of marine animal—perhaps a surviving plesiosaur, giant eel, or entirely novel creature.

The waters of British Columbia and Washington State provide a plausible habitat: deep, cold, nutrient-rich ocean with abundant fish populations, kelp forests, and thousands of islands creating sheltered bays and channels. The region's maritime culture and indigenous traditions both include long histories of large sea creature sightings, lending cultural depth to the modern Caddy phenomenon.

## Physical Description

### Body Structure

**Size:**
- Length: Typically 15-40 feet
- Some reports up to 60-70 feet
- Body diameter: 2-4 feet thick
- Estimated weight: 1,000-3,000+ pounds

**Body Shape:**
- Long, serpentine, flexible body
- Horizontal undulation (reptilian/fish-like)
- Multiple humps or coils visible when surfacing
- Usually 2-5 humps reported
- Cylindrical or slightly laterally compressed
- Extremely flexible spine

**Skin/Coloration:**
- Dark coloration most common:
  - Dark brown or black
  - Dark gray or bluish-gray
  - Greenish-brown
- Smooth, possibly rubbery texture
- Shiny or reflective when wet
- Possible mottling or patterns
- Some reports mention scales (minority)

### Head and Neck

**Head:**
- **Horse-like or camel-like** (most common description)
- Size: Comparable to horse or seal
- Elongated snout
- Large, prominent eyes
- Set on sides of head
- Dark eyes, intelligent appearance
- Wide mouth
- Possible whiskers or barbels (rare mentions)
- No visible teeth in most reports
- Nostril openings on top of snout

**Neck:**
- Long, slender, flexible neck
- 4-10 feet in length
- Thinner than body
- Can arch gracefully
- Swan-like or serpent-like flexibility
- Holds head well above water
- Can rotate head to observe

### Fins, Flippers, and Tail

**Front Appendages:**
- Two front flippers or fins
- Paddle or flipper-shaped
- Used for steering and propulsion
- Size: 2-4 feet long
- Visible when surfacing or turning

**Rear Appendages:**
- Possibly two rear flippers (less commonly seen)
- Or single tail structure

**Tail:**
- Long, tapering tail
- Possible flukes or horizontal tail fin (like cetacean)
- Or vertical tail fin (like fish)
- Descriptions vary
- Creates significant wake
- Sometimes breaks surface independently

**Dorsal Features:**
- Possible low dorsal ridge or fin
- Humps when surfacing (not true fins)
- Flexible body creating illusion of multiple segments

## Historical Accounts and Sightings

### Indigenous Traditions

**First Nations Legends:**
- **Coast Salish peoples:** Stories of Hiyitl'iik (sea serpent)
- **Nuu-chah-nulth:** Legends of large sea creatures
- **Haida and Tlingit:** Sea monster traditions
- **Cultural significance:** Powerful, respected beings
- **Spiritual aspects:** Guardians, sacred creatures
- **Taboos:** Proper respect required

**Artistic Representation:**
- Totem pole carvings
- Ceremonial art
- Oral traditions passed through generations
- Connection to broader water monster mythology

### Early European Contact (18th-19th Centuries)

**18th Century:**
- Captain George Vancouver's expeditions (1790s)
- Sailors' reports of large sea animals
- Attributed to whales, seals, or imagination
- No formal documentation

**19th Century Reports:**

**1850s-1860s:**
- Whaling ships and trading vessels report sightings
- Described as "sea serpents"
- Generally dismissed by authorities
- Local fishermen maintain sightings

**1897:**
- Victoria Daily Colonist newspaper reports sighting
- Witnesses describe 40-foot serpent-like creature
- Near Trial Island, BC

**Late 1800s:**
- Multiple reports from fishermen and sailors
- Consistency in descriptions emerging
- Still considered sailor's tales by mainstream

### Early 20th Century

**1905 - Multiple Witnesses:**
Crew of steamer Otter saw large serpent-like animal near Cadboro Bay. Described as 40+ feet, with horse-like head on long neck. Creature visible for several minutes.

**1932-1933 - Wave of Sightings:**

**October 1932:**
Major F.W. Kemp and his wife sighted creature in Cadboro Bay. Described in detail to newspapers. Sparked public interest.

**1933 - Multiple Sightings:**
Over a dozen reported sightings throughout year. Led newspaper editor Archie Wills to formally name creature "Cadborosaurus willsi" (portmanteau of Cadboro Bay + Greek *sauros* = lizard). Name and creature became cultural phenomenon.

**Kemp's Description (1933):**
"...about 80 feet long, with a head like a horse, a long sinuous body showing about eight 'coils' and a forked tail..."

**1933 - Victoria Sighting:**
Multiple witnesses including secretary of Victoria chamber of commerce saw creature. Described head and neck raised 10 feet above water. Moving through Strait with humped coils visible.

**1930s-1940s:**
- Regular sightings continue
- Creature becomes part of regional culture
- Skeptics attribute to misidentified whales, seals, or hoaxes
- Believers maintain unknown animal exists

### Mid-Late 20th Century

**1950s-1960s:**
- Sightings continue sporadically
- Scientific community generally dismissive
- Fishermen and locals maintain sightings
- Occasional newspaper features

**1977 - Haida Gwaii Sighting:**
Fishermen near Queen Charlotte Islands saw large serpent-like creature. Estimated 40 feet. Photographed but images unclear/distant.

**Notable 1980s-1990s Sightings:**

**1984 - Diver Encounter:**
Scuba diver near Hornby Island reported seeing large, long-necked animal underwater. Estimated 25-30 feet. Creature observed diver and swam away.

**1992 - Ferry Passengers:**
Multiple passengers on BC Ferry between Vancouver Island and mainland saw creature. Humps and long neck visible. Creature paced ferry briefly.

**1995 - Fishing Boat:**
Commercial fishermen encountered creature near Nootka Sound. Observed for 10+ minutes at close range (50 feet). Credible witnesses with decades of maritime experience.

### 21st Century Reports

**2000s:**
- Sightings continue at rate of 2-10 per year (reported)
- Video and photo attempts increase
- Social media allows quick dissemination
- Quality of evidence remains poor

**2009 - Kayaker Sighting:**
Kayaker in Gulf Islands saw large animal surface nearby. Described horse-like head, long neck, humps. Creature appeared curious but non-threatening. Submerged after 2-3 minutes.

**2011 - Cruise Ship Passengers:**
Multiple passengers on Alaska cruise ship reported sighting off Vancouver Island. Wake and dark shape visible. Too distant for clear identification.

**Recent Activity (2010s-2020s):**
- Reports continue
- Improved cameras but still no clear evidence
- Increased interest from cryptozoologists
- Tourist interest growing
- No scientific consensus or validation

## Physical Evidence

### Carcasses and Bodies

**Claimed Caddy Remains:**

**1937 - Naden Harbour Carcass:**
Most famous alleged Caddy specimen. Found inside sperm whale stomach at whaling station, Naden Harbour, Haida Gwaii. Photographed. Analysis:
- Photos show elongated carcass, approximately 10 feet
- Decomposed and partial
- Originally claimed as baby Caddy
- Later analysis (by marine biologists) identified as:
  - **Most likely: Decomposed fetal baleen whale**
  - Decomposition creates "pseudo-long-neck" appearance
  - Common pattern in marine carrion

**Other Alleged Carcasses:**
- Several decomposed marine animals found over decades
- All identified as known species upon examination:
  - Basking sharks (decompose to "plesiosaur-like" form)
  - Whales in various decomposition states
  - Large oarfish
  - Marine mammal remains

**No Validated Specimens:**
- No caddy carcass authenticated
- All examined specimens proven to be known animals
- Decomposition creates misleading appearances
- "Sea monster" frequently = decayed basking shark

### Photographic and Video Evidence

**Notable Images:**

**1937 Photo:**
Naden Harbour carcass photo widely circulated. Shows elongated form. As noted, later identified as whale fetus/stillbirth.

**Various Photos/Videos (20th-21st Century):**
- Dozens of photos and video clips
- All suffer from:
  - Distance (too far for detail)
  - Water conditions (glare, waves)
  - Low resolution
  - Motion blur
  - Ambiguous subjects

**Common Photographic Problems:**
- Shows "something" but not identifiable
- Could be seals, sea lions, driftwood, whales
- Kelp formations with current
- Boat wakes from other vessels
- No close-up, clear images exist

**Video Attempts:**
- Several videos posted online
- Analysis shows:
  - Known animals (seals, whales)
  - Floating debris
  - Artifacts (lens flares, etc.)
  - Hoaxes
- None conclusively show unknown animal

### Sonar and Technology

**Sonar Contacts:**
- Boaters occasionally report unusual sonar returns
- Large objects at various depths
- Difficult to verify:
  - Could be large fish, sharks, or marine mammals
  - Schools of fish
  - Kelp, logs, or other debris
  - Equipment anomalies
- No sustained tracking of putative Caddy

### Track/Sign Evidence

**Not Applicable:**
- Marine animal leaves no tracks
- No nests or lairs
- No feeding sign identifiable as unique
- Ocean habitat provides no trackable evidence

## Theories and Explanations

### Cryptozoological Hypotheses

**1. Surviving Plesiosaur:**
- Mesozoic marine reptile
- Long neck, flippers match descriptions
- Problems:
  - Extinct 66 million years ago
  - No fossil evidence in Pacific Northwest
  - Would need to breathe air (more frequent surfacing expected)
  - Cold-blooded (Northwest waters cold)
  - No breeding population evidence

**2. Surviving Basilosaurus:**
- Primitive whale, serpentine body form
- Eocene epoch (extinct 34 million years ago)
- Matches elongated body description
- Problems:
  - Massive extinction gap
  - Would breathe air (more visible)
  - Fossil record shows ocean, not coastal waters
  - No plausible survival mechanism

**3. Unknown Species of Elongated Seal:**
- Aberrant pinniped
- Some seals have elongated bodies
- Adapted to Pacific Northwest
- Problems:
  - No known seals match descriptions
  - Pinnipeds surface frequently to breathe
  - Would be common enough to identify
  - Breeding colonies required (where?)

**4. Giant Oarfish:**
- Oarfish (Regalecus glesne) reach 30-50 feet
- Serpentine, live in deep water
- Surface when sick or dying
- Horse-like head somewhat
- **Most plausible natural explanation**
- Problems:
  - Oarfish are deep-ocean, rarely inshore
  - Don't hold head above water as described
  - More ribbon-like than robust
  - Rare in Northeast Pacific

**5. Giant Eel:**
- Unknown species of enormous eel
- Pacific waters have various eel species
- Problems:
  - No known eels reach reported sizes
  - Morphology doesn't quite match
  - Would likely be caught by fisheries

**6. Unknown Marine Mammal:**
- Entirely novel cetacean or pinniped
- Evolved in Pacific
- Somehow avoided scientific documentation
- Problems:
  - Marine mammals well-studied
  - Breathing requirements
  - Breeding populations
  - No specimens or remains

**7. Whale Behavior Misinterpreted:**
- Gray whales, humpbacks, or orcas
- Unusual swimming positions
- Spyhopping, tail slapping
- Multiple whales appearing connected
- **Explains some sightings**
- Problems:
  - Doesn't match all descriptions
  - Witnesses familiar with whales
  - Shape discrepancies

### Skeptical Explanations

**Misidentified Known Animals:**

**Sea Lions and Seals:**
- California sea lions, Steller sea lions
- Harbor seals, elephant seals (rare)
- Can appear serpentine when swimming
- Multiple animals in line = "humps"
- Problems:
  - Local witnesses know these animals
  - Don't match descriptions well
  - Size discrepancies

**Whales:**
- Gray whales (migrate through area)
- Humpback whales
- Orcas (killer whales)
- Unusual behaviors can create illusions
- Spyhopping (vertical head-above-water)
- Problems:
  - Whales easily recognized usually
  - Blow (breathing spray) not reported with Caddy
  - Body shape different

**Oarfish:**
- Deep-sea fish, very elongated
- Rarely seen at surface
- Appear when dying or sick
- Serpentine appearance
- **Likely explanation for some sightings**
- Problems:
  - Unusual in inshore waters
  - Don't explain all sightings

**Kelp and Floating Debris:**
- Bull kelp (Nereocystis) forms long strands
- Logs and driftwood
- Current can animate debris
- Multiple logs in line resemble coils
- Problems:
  - Doesn't explain animated behavior
  - Witnesses report clear animal movement
  - Doesn't explain head/neck observations

**Sharks:**
- Basking sharks (large, surface-feeding)
- Sixgill sharks (deep water, occasionally surface)
- Unusual swimming postures
- Problems:
  - Morphology very different
  - Sharks well-known to fishermen
  - Doesn't match neck/head descriptions

**Hoaxes:**
- Some reports likely fabricated
- Economic incentive (tourism)
- Attention-seeking
- Deliberate fakes (models, doctored photos)
- Problems:
  - Doesn't explain centuries of reports
  - Too many independent, credible witnesses
  - Indigenous traditions predate modern hoaxing

**Mass Hysteria/Cultural Expectation:**
- Once legend established, people interpret ambiguous sights as Caddy
- Confirmation bias
- Cultural reinforcement
- Media influence
- Problems:
  - Many witnesses reluctant to report (fear of ridicule)
  - Consistent descriptions across long time period
  - Some very credible observers

### Moderate Skeptical View

**Composite Explanation (Most Likely):**
- No single unknown cryptid
- Multiple phenomena create "Caddy" legend:
  - **Oarfish** (rare sightings, most compelling)
  - **Elephant seals** (rare in area but possible)
  - **Whale behaviors** (spyhopping, unusual swimming)
  - **Sea lions in group** (creating multi-humped appearance)
  - **Kelp and debris** (inanimate misidentifications)
  - **Hoaxes and fabrications** (minority of cases)
  - **Cultural tradition** (reinforces interpretation)

This combination explains the range of sightings without requiring unknown animal.

## Scientific Perspective

### Marine Biology Assessment

**Why Scientists Skeptical:**

**Pacific Northwest Marine Environment:**
- Extremely well-studied
- Heavy fishing activity (commercial, recreational)
- Extensive scientific research
- Major shipping lanes
- No specimens ever captured
- No bones or remains found

**Population Requirements:**
- Breeding population needed (50-100+ individuals minimum)
- Genetic diversity requirements
- Such population would be encountered regularly
- Fisheries would catch specimens
- Carcasses would wash ashore periodically

**Lack of Physical Evidence:**
- No authenticated specimens
- No bones, teeth, or tissue samples
- All examined "carcasses" proven to be known animals
- No viable DNA samples
- No clear photographs despite ubiquitous cameras

**Official Position:**
- No credible evidence for unknown large marine animal
- All sightings explainable through known animals and phenomena
- Investigation not warranted without physical proof
- Caddy considered folklore, not zoological reality

### Academic Research

**Studies:**
- Few formal scientific studies
- Most investigation by cryptozoologists (not peer-reviewed)
- Some folklore and cultural studies
- Marine biology focuses on known species

**If Caddy Real:**
- Would overturn understanding of Pacific marine biodiversity
- Require explanation for how population avoided detection
- Scientific sensation
- Conservation implications
